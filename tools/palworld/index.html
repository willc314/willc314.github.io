How to actually land the passive
  • Only one child per generation matters. You need an offspring that is both the right species and carrying the passive. Everything else goes in the box.
  • Put the carrier in as the male. Game8's 100-egg test came out at 24% inheritance from the father against 20% from the mother, with 34% of eggs inheriting nothing.
  • Pair it with a mate that has zero passives. Junk traits compete for the four slots and drag your rate down hard.
  • Feed Special Cake. It raises the chance of passing parents' passives down. Avoid Extravagant Vegetable Cake — it pushes mutations, and a mutated egg is always a different species than the parents would normally make.
  • Two of the same species always breed that species. Once one target Pal has the passive, pair it with a plain one of its own kind and reroll the other three slots forever without risking the species again.
  • Watch the gender odds. A few Pals are lopsided — Bellanoir is 5% male, Beegarde and Elizabee 10%, Dazzi and Mozzarina 20%. Those rows get flagged in the chain.
What this tool knows and doesn't
  • Species is decided by floor((rankA + rankB + 1) / 2), then the closest Pal to that number. Low rank means rare — Aegidron sits at 30, Chikipi at 3080.
  • Only 183 of the 299 Pals can come out of that formula. The other 116 are exactly the ones the combo table gives an explicit same-species row to — most elemental subspecies, plus legendaries and tower and raid bosses. Those rows exist because the formula can no longer produce those Pals: without one, breeding two of them together would hand you something else entirely. All 114 rows check out as necessary, which is what pins the rule down. Three subspecies — Kelpsea Ignis, Shroomer Noct and Wumpo Botan — have no such row, and that is because they don’t need one: they never left the formula pool, so they breed normally and make themselves.
  • Unique combos are the 251 fixed pairings that ignore rank entirely. Two are gender-dependent: Katress × Wixen gives Wixen Noct or Katress Ignis depending on which parent is female.
  • Ties are not resolved here. 179 of the possible averages sit exactly between two candidates, and the data table offers nothing to break them — every formula-eligible Pal's priority value is just its rank, so it carries no extra information. Those steps get flagged with both candidates named, and Exact landings only (on by default) avoids them entirely.
  • The tree reads top-down. The finished Pal is at the top; the two Pals directly beneath it are its parents, and so on down. Anything with nothing beneath it is a leaf you catch or already own. Cards with a highlighted border are the line carrying your passive.
  • My box counts generations, not eggs. Every row is one successful hatch; how many eggs that takes is a dice roll the tool doesn't model. It finds the plan with the fewest hatches by merging same-species carriers first (species-safe, so it's free of risk), chaining each group to the target once, then joining at the end. It does not try breeding two different-species carriers into some intermediate species — that could occasionally be shorter, so treat the number as an upper bound.
  • The passive list is 155 entries, filtered from the skill table by dropping the gear and accessory variants. A few borderline ones may still be equipment-only rather than things a Pal can carry.
  • 1 Pal has no breeding recipe at all — Panthalus. It sits outside the formula pool, no combo produces it, and it has no same-species row, so even a matched pair will not breed true. Catch-only in both directions.
